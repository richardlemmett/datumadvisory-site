Investigating electrical failures at a new hospital

Repeated component failures across a hospital development required an investigation into both the equipment and its operating conditions, followed by a coordinated programme of repairs.

Repeated failures across a new development

I was asked to investigate problems affecting a large number of electrical distribution panels at a new hospital development in London. I travelled to site with tools and a set of schematics I had not previously seen.

Following the site induction, we examined the affected equipment. Several components had failed or were damaged, with a recurring pattern of burnt-out parts. As we checked more panels, similar damage appeared elsewhere.

Looking beyond the damaged components

The repeated pattern prompted a wider investigation. I made enquiries about site operations and asked to examine the site telemetry data.

We concluded that overnight generator testing under low-load conditions was causing overvoltage events across the network. The instrumentation had recorded a peak of 489 V, and the more voltage-sensitive components had suffered damage.

The investigation connected the condition of the panels with what was happening elsewhere in the electrical installation. Replacing the failed components needed to be accompanied by action on the supply conditions.

Coordinating the correction and repairs

The client took responsibility for correcting the overvoltage situation and arranged for the transformer taps to be adjusted.

I prepared a plan to test and repair the damaged panels, obtained the required spare parts and returned to site several times to complete the work. The issues were spread across 115 panels in four cores and 15 floors, with no usable lifts during the work.

All identified issues were resolved to the client’s satisfaction.

115 panels across four cores and 15 floors

A planned programme of testing, repairs and follow-up visits, alongside the client’s correction of the supply conditions.

What this demonstrates for a business

A fault investigation needs to explain why the failure occurred and establish what must change. Physical evidence, technical documents, operating conditions and recorded information can each provide part of the answer.

This case shows how I approach root cause analysis and technical investigation: examine the detail, follow the evidence across system boundaries, establish the actions needed and organise the follow-through.

For Datum, that experience informs work on recurring quality problems, process failures, unreliable information and systems issues. Findings need to give the business a clear basis for corrective action.
