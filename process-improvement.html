Business process improvement and manufacturing support

When work takes too long or the same problems keep coming back, it helps to look across the whole process. I work with businesses to understand what is getting in the way and develop improvements they can put into practice.

Recognising the problem

Orders wait for information. Production plans change repeatedly. People enter the same details into several systems. A handover relies on one person knowing what to do.

These are useful starting points for investigation. We look at how work moves, what decisions are needed and where time, information or capacity is being lost.

How I can help

  • Map activities, handovers and responsibilities with the people doing the work.
  • Identify constraints, recurring causes and conflicting requirements.
  • Review production information, stock control, documentation and supporting systems.
  • Develop practical changes and a manageable sequence for introducing them.
  • Agree measures that show whether the change is helping.

Experience in Lean manufacturing, the Toyota Production System and Six Sigma informs the approach. The investigation determines which methods are useful.

Systems that support the way work needs to happen

A process improvement may involve clearer responsibilities, different working arrangements, training or better use of an existing system. Where a tool is needed, its purpose follows from the work it needs to support.

My background includes manufacturing processes, engineering, quality, commercial work and business systems. This helps connect a local improvement with its effects on other departments.

What we could agree to deliver

A process review with findings and priorities; a revised workflow; clearer roles and controls; an agreed improvement trial; or support coordinating implementation.

Related experience

The SAP manufacturing programme and the telecommunications rollout show how operational arrangements, information and systems were brought together.
