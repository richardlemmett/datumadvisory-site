Career case study · Before Datum

Waste, resource efficiency and environmental data

Environmental responsibilities connect with everyday decisions about materials, energy, costs, processes and information. This example draws on my experience at Schneider Electric.

Practical environmental responsibility

During my career at Schneider Electric, my responsibilities included recycling, waste management, environmental reporting, energy monitoring and reduction programmes, emissions monitoring, and regulatory review and permitting.

Working in a global manufacturing business helped shape how I approach problems: understand the process, examine the evidence, involve the people with relevant knowledge and follow the work through.

These responsibilities connected environmental performance with production, purchasing, facilities, finance and management systems.

Organising more than 40 waste streams

Working with my team, we established waste optimisation and recycling arrangements covering more than 40 waste streams. Most were revenue generating or cost neutral.

The result brought resource recovery and commercial considerations together. Understanding the materials leaving the business was part of understanding the operation itself.

More than 40 waste streams

Monitoring that supports decisions

Energy reduction programmes, emissions monitoring and environmental reporting all depend on usable information. The records need to connect with the processes, equipment and activities they describe.

My responsibilities across these areas reinforced the importance of clear ownership, consistent records and practical monitoring arrangements. Information gathered for reporting can also help people identify waste, investigate changes and decide where to focus improvement.

The connection with sustainability reporting today

A business preparing Scope 1, Scope 2 or Scope 3 emissions information may need to bring together energy records, purchasing information, transport data, waste records and information from suppliers. The practical challenge can be finding the records, agreeing what they mean and collecting them consistently.

Datum can help map the information required, identify sources and owners, establish collection and checking arrangements, and develop repeatable reporting tools. Where specialist carbon-accounting methods or assurance are needed, that work can be coordinated with the appropriate specialists.

This sits within Datum’s wider work on processes, information and business systems. Environmental requirements are considered alongside operational needs, costs and the organisation’s ability to sustain the work.
