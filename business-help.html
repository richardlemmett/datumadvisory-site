Business improvement and systems advice for SMEs

Something in the business needs attention. You can see the symptoms, but working out the cause and what to do next is harder. Datum helps you make sense of the problem and agree a practical response.

A useful starting point

You may be spending too much time preparing reports, struggling to coordinate work between departments or considering software without a clear set of requirements. A larger customer may be asking for controls and evidence you have never needed before.

You can start with what is happening. I work with you and the people involved to understand the need, examine the options and establish what would be worth doing.

Connecting the different parts of your business

Decisions about customers, products, processes, money and technology affect each other. I bring experience across operations, engineering, commercial, finance, systems and IT to help people understand those connections.

That can mean interpreting a technical proposal for an owner, helping IT understand a production requirement or bringing sales and manufacturing together around a workable commitment.

Help as the business develops

A small specialist business might need help with a pre-qualification questionnaire (PQQ), a customer cyber security questionnaire or preparations for ISO 9001. A growing manufacturer might need more consistent costing, stock information or production reporting.

As the challenge becomes broader, the work may involve investment decisions, management responsibilities and coordinating several improvements. Each engagement is shaped around the circumstances. A focused piece of work can be the complete answer.

What an engagement gives you

We agree the intended result before chargeable work starts. Depending on the need, this could be written findings, a requirements brief, an improvement plan, a working reporting tool, a supplier assessment or support through implementation.

The value comes from establishing what needs attention, making informed decisions and organising work so that progress is achievable. Advice can lead to better use of existing resources, a smaller intervention or a well-founded investment.
